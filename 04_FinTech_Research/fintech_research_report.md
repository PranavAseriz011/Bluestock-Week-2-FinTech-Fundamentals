# FinTech Business Research Report

## 1. Introduction

Financial Technology (FinTech) refers to the use of technology to improve and automate financial services. In India, FinTech companies provide services such as online stock trading, digital payments, investment platforms, banking services, and financial data analysis.

Stock market platforms allow users to buy and sell securities through digital applications. Behind these applications, software systems process market data, manage transactions, maintain customer records, and generate reports.

Data analytics plays an important role in this ecosystem by helping companies understand customer behaviour, monitor business performance, identify trends, and improve the overall user experience.

## 3. Indian Brokerage Platforms

A brokerage platform is a service that allows investors and traders to buy and sell financial securities through a stock exchange. Modern brokerage platforms provide mobile applications, websites, market information, order placement, portfolio tracking, and transaction reports.

Examples of Indian brokerage platforms include:

- Zerodha
- Groww
- Upstox
- Angel One
- ICICI Direct

These platforms use software systems to process orders, display market information, maintain customer records, and provide analytical insights.

### 3.1 What Is a Demat Account?

A Demat account, short for Dematerialized Account, holds securities such as shares and exchange-traded funds in electronic form.

When an investor purchases shares, the securities are credited to their Demat account after the applicable settlement process. When shares are sold, the securities are debited according to the transaction and settlement requirements.

A Demat account differs from a trading account:

- **Demat account:** Holds securities electronically.
- **Trading account:** Used to place buy and sell orders through a broker.
- **Bank account:** Used to transfer funds for investment transactions.

These accounts serve different purposes but work together during the investment process.

### 3.2 Depositories: NSDL and CDSL

India has two major securities depositories:

- **NSDL:** National Securities Depository Limited.
- **CDSL:** Central Depository Services (India) Limited.

Depositories maintain securities in electronic form and support the transfer and settlement of securities through their systems and participants.

Investors generally access depository services through a Depository Participant (DP), such as an eligible broker or financial institution.

### 3.3 Why These Systems Matter for Data Analysts

Brokerage platforms and depositories generate data related to orders, transactions, holdings, settlements, and customer activity.

Data analysts may use this information to:

- Measure trading activity and platform usage.
- Analyse transaction volumes and operational performance.
- Identify failed orders or settlement exceptions.
- Understand customer engagement.
- Build dashboards and business reports.

Financial data must be handled securely, accurately, and in accordance with applicable regulations.

## 4. Role of SEBI

The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) is the regulatory authority responsible for regulating India's securities market and protecting investors' interests.

SEBI works to promote fair practices, improve market transparency, and regulate market intermediaries such as stockbrokers and other registered entities.

Its major responsibilities include:

- Protecting investors from unfair market practices.
- Regulating securities markets and market intermediaries.
- Promoting transparency and orderly market operations.
- Supporting investor awareness and education.
- Taking action against violations of securities market regulations.

SEBI plays an important role in maintaining trust and confidence in India's financial markets.

## 5. Trading Lifecycle

The trading lifecycle describes the major stages involved when an investor buys or sells securities.

### 5.1 Main Stages

1. **Account Setup:** The investor opens the required trading and Demat accounts and completes applicable verification.
2. **Order Placement:** The investor places a buy or sell order through a brokerage platform.
3. **Order Routing:** The broker routes the order to the relevant stock exchange.
4. **Order Matching:** The exchange matches compatible buy and sell orders according to its trading rules.
5. **Trade Execution:** Once matched, the trade is executed and the transaction details are recorded.
6. **Clearing:** The clearing process determines the obligations of the parties involved in the trade.
7. **Settlement:** Funds and securities are transferred according to the applicable settlement cycle and rules.
8. **Account Update:** The investor's funds or securities records are updated as applicable.

The exact process and timing can vary depending on the security, transaction type, and applicable market rules.

### 5.2 Clearing and Settlement

**Clearing** determines the obligations arising from executed trades.

**Settlement** involves completing those obligations by transferring funds and securities through the relevant systems.

For many equity trades in India, the standard settlement cycle is T+1, meaning settlement generally occurs on the next working day after the trade date. Applicable rules and exceptions may differ by transaction type.

## 6. Major Market Participants

The Indian securities market includes several participants who perform different functions.

| Participant | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Investors | Buy and sell securities to meet their financial objectives. |
| Traders | Buy and sell securities based on their strategies or requirements. |
| Stockbrokers | Provide access to stock exchanges and facilitate client orders. |
| Stock Exchanges | Provide regulated marketplaces for trading securities. |
| Depositories | Hold securities electronically and facilitate their transfer. |
| Depository Participants | Provide investors access to depository services. |
| Clearing Corporations | Manage clearing and settlement obligations. |
| SEBI | Regulates the securities market and protects investor interests. |
| Listed Companies | Issue securities and disclose relevant financial information. |

Understanding these participants helps data analysts interpret trading, transaction, settlement, and operational datasets.

## 7. Case Study: Zerodha and Data Analytics

### 7.1 Company Overview

Zerodha is an Indian financial services company known for its online stockbroking platform. It provides technology-based access to financial markets and tools that help customers monitor investments and trading activity.

Digital brokerage platforms generate large amounts of operational and financial data through customer interactions, orders, transactions, and account activity.

### 7.2 How Data Analytics Can Support a Brokerage Platform

Data analytics can help a brokerage platform understand its operations, improve services, and make informed business decisions.

The following are examples of how analytics can be applied in a brokerage business. They describe potential applications, not claims about Zerodha's undisclosed internal systems.

#### 1. Customer Behaviour Analysis**

Analysts can study application usage, feature engagement, and customer support requests to understand how customers interact with the platform.

#### 2. Platform Performance Monitoring**

Operational dashboards can track response times, application errors, failed requests, and service availability. These metrics help technology teams identify performance problems.

#### 3. Trading Activity Analysis**

Aggregated trading data can help business teams understand transaction volumes, peak usage periods, and changes in customer activity.

#### 4. Customer Support Analytics

Support teams can analyse ticket categories, resolution times, and recurring complaints to identify areas where customer service can improve.

#### 5. Business Performance Reporting

Business intelligence dashboards can summarise customer activity, operational costs, service quality, and other business indicators to support management decisions.

### 7.3 Example Analytics Dashboard

A brokerage platform could build a dashboard containing the following metrics:

| Metric | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Daily Active Users | Measures daily platform engagement. |
| Order Success Rate | Tracks the proportion of orders successfully processed or accepted under the defined metric. |
| Average API Response Time | Measures application responsiveness. |
| Support Ticket Resolution Time | Tracks customer support efficiency. |
| Application Error Rate | Helps monitor software reliability. |
| Customer Retention Rate | Measures how well the platform retains customers over time. |

These metrics should have clearly defined calculation rules, reporting periods, and data-quality checks.

### 7.4 Benefits of Data Analytics

Data analytics can help brokerage platforms:

- Improve application reliability and responsiveness.
- Identify recurring customer problems.
- Make customer support more efficient.
- Monitor business and operational performance.
- Support evidence-based product decisions.
- Identify unusual operational patterns for further investigation.

Financial and personal data must be handled securely, with appropriate access controls and privacy safeguards.
